# Common Graph Logo — Brainstorming Notes

Working document for eventual graphic artist collaboration.  Not a finished brief — just captured thinking to start from.

## Constraints and goals

- **Works at all sizes** — from favicon (16px) to sticker to conference slide backdrop.
- **Works monochrome** — should read clearly in a single color (black on white, white on black, or any single-color print).
- **Language-agnostic** — no letters, no typography, no "CG" monogram.  Should be recognizable to any audience regardless of language.
- **Unrelated to the name** — the mark should stand on its own, not depend on or reference the name "Common Graph."
- **Simpler than Reading Rainbow Library logo** — that logo (for reference) is narratively rich with books, trees, campfire, rainbow, mug.  Works beautifully at stamp/badge scale but too complex for a modern digital-first mark.  CG needs something with fewer elements.
- **Can have some warmth / narrative quality** — doesn't need to be cold geometric minimalism.  Organic, hand-feel elements welcome.

## Aesthetic directions Joshua is open to

All four tonal registers are acceptable, not decided:
- Technical-minimal (tidy geometric)
- Academic-institutional (bookish, gravitas)
- Playful-approachable (welcoming, character-led)
- Classic-timeless

## Reference aesthetic

Reading Rainbow Library logo (Joshua's existing library brand):
- Monochrome
- Rich narrative: books, tree, campfire, pine, rainbow arc, steaming mug
- Rainbow text arc doubles as the visual rainbow
- Illustrative, pictorial
- Warm, literary feeling
- Works beautifully at stamp scale

Joshua loves it but knows CG needs something fundamentally simpler.

## Conceptual anchors (what the logo could evoke)

From the project's architecture:
- **Frames** — the core semantic primitive (but not meant to be taken literally as visual frames/boxes)
- **Items cohering around an anchor** — frames all about the same thing
- **Nodes connected by relationships** — the graph structure
- **Trust web / social graph** — small-world network of endorsements
- **Peer-to-peer** — distributed nodes
- **Commons** — shared, open, universal
- **Semantic / meaning** — structure, grounding, foundation

## Concept directions (cast wide net)

### Structural / graph-theoretic

1. **Minimal graph cluster** — 5-7 dots connected by lines.  Risk of being generic; needs a distinctive composition to avoid looking like a stock network icon.
2. **Node cohering around center** — a central filled circle with 5-6 curved lines radiating outward, each ending in a smaller unfilled circle.  Not straight spokes — lines bend gently like tendrils or grass.  Slightly asymmetric composition.  Reads as seedItem-head / dandelion-spore, but literally depicts "item with frames cohering around it."
3. **Hexagonal lattice fragment** — honeycomb pattern, suggests repeatable structure.
4. **Atom-like orbit** — central node with surrounding ring of nodes.  Clean symmetry, but risks feeling generic.

### Organic / living

5. **Flower / mandala** — radial symmetry, 6 or 8 petals each a small graph connection.  Natural + structured.
6. **Seed or sprout** — abstract shape suggesting foundation / beginning.  Could have internal branching structure visible, like the inside of a pomegranate or a dandelion seedItem.
7. **Woven thread / knot** — three or more curves that interlock.  Suggests unity through connection.  Celtic-knot energy without being Celtic-specific.
8. **Coral or branching growth** — fractal-ish form, suggests organic emergence of structure.

### Symbolic / archetypal

9. **Compass rose** — universal, directional.  Could have internal graph structure as cardinal elements.
10. **Star / spark** — simple origin / new light feeling.  Can be geometric or hand-drawn.
11. **Keystone / arch** — evokes "foundation" if paired with supporting structure shadow.
12. **Web / constellation** — sparse dots connected like stars.  Small-world / trust graph feel.

### Specific vivid concepts (expansions of promising directions)

**A. The Cohering Node** (expansion of #2)
A central filled circle about pea-sized.  Five or six slightly curved lines radiate outward, each terminating in a smaller unfilled circle.  Curves bend gently like tendrils or grass reaching.  Deliberately asymmetric (e.g., four lines going up-and-out, two going down) so it doesn't feel sterile.  Reads as a seedItem-head or dandelion-spore.  Literally depicts the CG item model.

**B. The Hearth Graph** (expansion of #5 and #10)
Inspired by the warmth in Reading Rainbow.  A central small shape (dot, flame, spark) with three to five lines arcing outward like embers drifting from a fire.  Some lines terminate in dots, some trail into space.  Organic and warm.  Reads as "node with connections" AND "warmth radiating outward."  Good at any size.

**C. Three and One** (expansion of #7)
Three filled dots arranged in a loose triangle, curving lines connecting each pair.  In the negative space at the center, a fourth shape emerges — leaf, flame, or intentional void.  Three dots are "participants"; the shape that emerges from their connection is "meaning."  Layered semantic: graph structure + emergent meaning.

**D. The Small World** (expansion of #12)
Eight or nine scattered dots arranged roughly (not perfectly) in a circle.  Sparse connecting lines — not all pairs connected.  Reads as constellation or trust graph.  Distinctively "social network" without corporate org-chart feel.

## Joshua's reactions so far

- Likes the direction overall, but uncertain which to pursue.
- Specifically interested in avoiding the typographic approach (no letters, no monogram).
- Open to all tonal registers.
- Wants something unrelated to the literal name.
- Notes: the "frame" concept shouldn't be taken literally as a visual frame/box.

## Strongest candidates (initial assessment)

- **Concept A (Cohering Node)** — strongest if goal is directly reflecting architecture.
- **Concept B (Hearth Graph)** — strongest if goal is narrative warmth echoing Reading Rainbow's feel.
- Potential combination: a cohering node with *warmth* in the central element.

## Open questions for the artist

- Color treatment: pure monochrome only, or allow a single accent color?
- Does the logo work as a standalone mark, or does it need to pair with a wordmark?
- Animated variants (for the website): should the mark lend itself to subtle motion, or stay static?
- Should the mark read as "seal / stamp / badge" (circular, contained) or "sigil / mark" (freely composed)?
